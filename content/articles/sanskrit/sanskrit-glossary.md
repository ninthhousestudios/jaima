<p><br>This is a glossary of Sanskrit astrological terms.</p><p>The format of entries is as follows:</p><p>(<strong>English way of writing the word</strong>) - (Devanagari) - (IAST) - (Kyoto-Harvard)<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-1" href="#footnote-1" target="_self">1</a> - definition</p><p>Devanagari is the usual way of writing Sanskrit in India today.</p><p>IAST is the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alphabet_of_Sanskrit_Transliteration">International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration</a>, a precise way of indicating Sanskrit text in a Roman alphabet using diacritics.</p><p><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard-Kyoto">Kyoto-Harvard</a> is another transliteration system that doesn’t use diacritics, so it can be typed easily with a normal keyboard.</p><p>Sanskrit words are given in their stem form, i.e., the form you will find listed in Monier-Williams. You can look them up there to find their gender and declension.</p><p>I am including a KH version so that if you would like to look up the word in an online Sanskrit dictionary yourself you can do so easily. <a href="https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/">Cologne University</a> has a number of digital Sanskrit dictionaries you can choose from. I usually use <a href="https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/2020/web/webtc/indexcaller.php">Monier-Williams</a> (MW). The word you want to look up goes in the “citation” box. Make sure the “input” drop down is set to “Kyoto-Harvard”. The “output” will give the definitino in IAST if set to “Roman Unicode”. </p><p>I found <a href="https://learnsanskrit.cc/">this dictionary</a> as well, which is more user-friendly, and also has an English-Sanskrit option. One of the challenges of interpreting Sanskrit words that aren’t well transliterated<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-2" href="#footnote-2" target="_self">2</a> are that sometimes there are many options. One English letter may stand in for two or three possible Sanskrit letters. With the Cologne dictionaries, you have to type just the right word, whereas this dictionary has more interpretive features, so you can maybe be off and still find a word, which has been very helpful to me.</p><p>The Devanagari and IAST text on this page was generated using <a href="https://www.yesvedanta.com/transliterate/">this tool</a>.</p><p>If you have potential corrections or requests, leave a comment or send me an email. Likewise, there are some words that I have not been able to figure out, so their definitions are marked (???).
